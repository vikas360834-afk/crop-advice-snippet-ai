# EXPANDED AGRICULTURAL CORPUS - FARMING BEST PRACTICES & WEATHER ADVICE

[CROP: Wheat | CONTEXT: rust disease | WEATHER: moist, high humidity 85%, temperature 18-22C | SOIL: all soil types | STAGE: flag leaf stage] ADVICE: High humidity and mild temperatures strongly favor stripe and leaf rust spore germination. Spray tebuconazole or propiconazole at 0.1% immediately upon detecting yellow or brown pustules. Ensure 18 to 20 cm row spacing to maintain canopy ventilation.
[CROP: Wheat | CONTEXT: terminal heat stress | WEATHER: heatwave, dry hot winds, temperature >32C | SOIL: sandy loam | STAGE: grain filling] ADVICE: Sudden heatwaves during grain filling cause premature drying and shriveled grains. Apply light sprinkler irrigation during calm late evening hours and foliar spray 0.5% potassium chloride or 2% potassium nitrate to buffer thermal shock.
[CROP: Wheat | CONTEXT: drought and water deficit | WEATHER: prolonged dry spell, rainfall absent | SOIL: light sandy soil | STAGE: crown root initiation] ADVICE: Crown root initiation at 20-25 days after sowing is the most critical moisture phase. Provide life-saving irrigation and apply straw mulch at 3 tons per hectare to retain soil moisture.
[CROP: Wheat | CONTEXT: unseasonal heavy rain, waterlogging | WEATHER: intense rainfall, cloudy overcast | SOIL: heavy clay soil | STAGE: tillering] ADVICE: Dig surface drainage channels between beds immediately to prevent hypoxic root asphyxiation. Withhold urea application until soil drains, then apply foliar urea spray 1% to jumpstart nitrogen uptake.
[CROP: Wheat | CONTEXT: broadleaf weed competition | WEATHER: dry sunny calm weather, wind <5 km/h | SOIL: loamy soil | STAGE: 30-35 days post sowing] ADVICE: Spray metsulfuron-methyl 20% WP at 20 grams per hectare or 2,4-D amine when broadleaf weeds are at the 2 to 4 leaf stage. Never spray in windy conditions to avoid drift onto neighboring sensitive broadleaf crops.
[CROP: Wheat | CONTEXT: lodging prevention | WEATHER: gusty winds, rainy forecast | SOIL: fertile alluvial soil with high nitrogen | STAGE: stem elongation] ADVICE: Apply plant growth regulator chlormequat chloride (CCC) at 1.5 liters per hectare at first node stage to thicken stem walls and prevent lodging during seasonal squalls.
[CROP: Wheat | CONTEXT: loose smut | WEATHER: cool moist spring | SOIL: fertile well-drained | STAGE: seed treatment] ADVICE: Treat seeds with carboxin 37.5% + thiram 37.5% at 2.5 grams per kg seed before sowing to destroy dormant internal mycelium inside embryo tissues.

[CROP: Rice | CONTEXT: blast disease | WEATHER: high humidity >90%, intermittent drizzles, overcast skies | SOIL: clay loam | STAGE: tillering to panicle initiation] ADVICE: High atmospheric humidity with overcast days accelerates blast lesion development. Apply tricyclazole 75 WP at 0.6 g/liter or azoxystrobin. Temporarily drain standing water from paddies and suspend nitrogen top-dressing until fungal spread halts.
[CROP: Rice | CONTEXT: drought and water shortage | WEATHER: delayed monsoon, low rainfall | SOIL: clay soil | STAGE: vegetative] ADVICE: Switch from continuous submergence to Alternate Wetting and Drying (AWD). Install perforated field water tubes and re-irrigate only when water depth drops to 15 cm below ground surface, conserving up to 30% irrigation water.
[CROP: Rice | CONTEXT: flash floods and submergence | WEATHER: torrential monsoon rains, water depth >50 cm | SOIL: alluvial silt | STAGE: seedling transplanting] ADVICE: Plant flood-tolerant varieties carrying the Sub1 gene (such as Swarna-Sub1). In submerged fields, drain floodwater gradually to prevent sudden seedling collapse and apply 20 kg potash per hectare once water recedes.
[CROP: Rice | CONTEXT: heat-induced spikelet sterility | WEATHER: heatwave, midday temperature >35C | SOIL: silty clay | STAGE: flowering anthesis] ADVICE: High temperature during morning anthesis causes pollen desiccation and spikelet sterility. Maintain a 5 cm water layer in paddies to reduce canopy microclimate temperature by 2 to 3 degrees Celsius.
[CROP: Rice | CONTEXT: stem borer attack | WEATHER: warm humid evenings, temperature 25-30C | SOIL: clayey soil | STAGE: tillering] ADVICE: Monitor adult yellow stem borer moths using light and pheromone traps at 20 traps per hectare. Apply cartap hydrochloride 4G granules at 25 kg/ha or chlorantraniliprole in standing water upon seeing 5% dead hearts.
[CROP: Rice | CONTEXT: zinc deficiency khaira disease | WEATHER: submerged cold soils, waterlogged | SOIL: alkaline calcareous soil pH >7.8 | STAGE: nursery and early transplanting] ADVICE: Cold waterlogged alkaline conditions immobilize soil zinc. Foliar spray 0.5% zinc sulfate heptahydrate neutralized with 0.25% calcium hydroxide twice at 15 and 30 days after transplanting.
[CROP: Rice | CONTEXT: bacterial leaf blight | WEATHER: stormy winds, high rainfall, typhoon conditions | SOIL: fertile soil | STAGE: maximum tillering] ADVICE: Strong winds create leaf micro-wounds where bacterial pathogens enter. Spray copper hydroxide 77 WP at 2 g/liter combined with validamycin. Avoid excess nitrogen topdressing during storm alerts.

[CROP: Tomato | CONTEXT: early blight Alternaria | WEATHER: warm humid weather, temperature 24-29C, frequent morning dew | SOIL: sandy loam | STAGE: flowering and early fruiting] ADVICE: Heavy morning dews combined with warm temperatures trigger rapid early blight lesion spread. Prune all bottom leaves within 30 cm of ground level, lay black plastic mulch, and apply preventive chlorothalonil or mancozeb every 7 to 10 days.
[CROP: Tomato | CONTEXT: late blight Phytophthora | WEATHER: cool foggy conditions, persistent drizzle, temperature 15-20C | SOIL: clay soil | STAGE: vegetative to fruiting] ADVICE: Cool wet foggy spells trigger destructive late blight outbreaks. Spray systemic metalaxyl plus mancozeb or cymoxanil upon first weather warning. Avoid overhead sprinkler irrigation; always use drip lines.
[CROP: Tomato | CONTEXT: blossom end rot | WEATHER: hot dry winds, erratic watering, high evaporation | SOIL: light sandy soil | STAGE: fruit development] ADVICE: Rapid fruit growth under hot dry conditions induces localized calcium transport deficiency. Provide uniform drip irrigation twice daily and spray calcium chloride 0.5% or calcium nitrate on expanding fruit clusters.
[CROP: Tomato | CONTEXT: flower and bud drop | WEATHER: extreme heatwave, temperature >35C, low humidity | SOIL: loamy soil | STAGE: peak flowering] ADVICE: Extreme midday temperatures kill pollen viability leading to flower abortion. Install 35% agro-shade nets overhead and spray 15 ppm naphthalene acetic acid (NAA) in early mornings to stimulate fruit retention.
[CROP: Tomato | CONTEXT: bacterial wilt Ralstonia | WEATHER: hot waterlogged conditions, temperature >30C | SOIL: acidic clayey soil with poor drainage | STAGE: vegetative] ADVICE: Bacterial wilt flourishes in hot, wet soils. Plant tomatoes on 25 cm raised beds, amend soil with agricultural lime to bring pH to 6.5, and drench rhizosphere with biological antagonist Pseudomonas fluorescens at 10 g/liter.
[CROP: Tomato | CONTEXT: whitefly and leaf curl virus | WEATHER: dry warm weather, temperature 28-34C | SOIL: well-drained loam | STAGE: seedling to vegetative] ADVICE: Whitefly vectors multiply rapidly in dry warm weather. Erect yellow sticky traps at canopy level and spray 5% neem seed kernel extract or acetamiprid 20 SP at 0.3 g/liter to halt virus transmission.
[CROP: Tomato | CONTEXT: frost freeze protection | WEATHER: cold wave, sub-zero frost forecast, night temperature <3C | SOIL: loam | STAGE: all stages] ADVICE: Run light drip or micro-sprinkler irrigation prior to midnight; freezing water releases latent heat buffering soil temperature. Cover beds with non-woven polypropylene fleece row covers.

[CROP: Potato | CONTEXT: late blight epidemic | WEATHER: cool wet weather, relative humidity >90%, temperature 12-19C | SOIL: loam | STAGE: tuber bulking] ADVICE: Extended cloudy wet periods are prime late blight triggers. Apply dimethomorph plus mancozeb or fenamidone. Earthing-up soil high around plants forms an effective physical barrier protecting growing tubers from fungal spore wash.
[CROP: Potato | CONTEXT: common scab | WEATHER: dry soil conditions during tuber initiation | SOIL: alkaline dry sandy soil pH >6.5 | STAGE: tuberization 4-6 weeks after planting] ADVICE: Streptomyces scabies bacteria infect expanding tuber lenticels under dry conditions. Maintain continuous soil moisture at 75-80% field capacity for 5 weeks starting at tuber initiation, and avoid applying fresh farmyard manure or lime just prior to planting.
[CROP: Potato | CONTEXT: frost injury | WEATHER: clear sky, calm wind, freezing night temperature <2C | SOIL: sandy loam | STAGE: early vegetative canopy] ADVICE: Cold air pooling on calm clear nights freezes tender potato foliage. Apply evening furrow irrigation and burn damp crop residue along the field border to create an insulating smoke blanket.
[CROP: Potato | CONTEXT: aphid vector explosion | WEATHER: dry warm spring, temperature 22-26C | SOIL: all types | STAGE: seed potato crop] ADVICE: Warm dry spells accelerate peach-potato aphid reproduction spreading potato leafroll virus. Spray thiamethoxam 25 WG at 0.2 g/liter and dehaulm vines 12 days before harvest when aphid count crosses critical threshold.
[CROP: Potato | CONTEXT: potassium nutrition for tuber size | WEATHER: normal sunny weather | SOIL: sandy loam | STAGE: tuber bulking] ADVICE: Tuber bulking demands high potassium. Apply sulfate of potash (SOP) at 120 kg K2O per hectare rather than muriate of potash to enhance tuber dry matter, reduce bruising, and extend cold storage shelf life.

[CROP: Maize | CONTEXT: fall armyworm Spodoptera | WEATHER: warm dry spells followed by rain, temperature 26-32C | SOIL: well-drained loam | STAGE: whorl stage V4 to V8] ADVICE: Scout central whorls for early pinhole damage and moist frass. Drop granular sand-ash mixture or apply emamectin benzoate 5 SG at 0.4 g/liter or spinetoram directly into whorls during dawn or dusk when larvae are active.
[CROP: Maize | CONTEXT: drought stress during pollination | WEATHER: severe drought, temperature >34C | SOIL: sandy loam | STAGE: tasseling and silking] ADVICE: Tasseling and silking are the most drought-vulnerable stages; water stress causes pollen-silk desynchronization leading to barren ears. Prioritize every available liter of irrigation during this two-week window.
[CROP: Maize | CONTEXT: waterlogging and root rot | WEATHER: continuous torrential rains | SOIL: heavy clay soil with low percolation | STAGE: early seedling V2] ADVICE: Maize seedlings cannot tolerate standing water beyond 24 hours. Construct deep inter-row drainage furrows, earth up plants to aerate adventitious roots, and apply foliar spray of 19-19-19 water soluble fertilizer at 5 g/liter.
[CROP: Maize | CONTEXT: phosphorus fixation | WEATHER: cold wet spring soil | SOIL: acidic red or lateritic soil pH <5.5 | STAGE: sowing] ADVICE: Phosphorus mobility stalls in cold acidic soils. Apply agricultural limestone 4 weeks prior to sowing and band diammonium phosphate (DAP) 5 cm to the side and 5 cm below seed depth to maximize root contact.

[CROP: Cotton | CONTEXT: bollworm Helicoverpa | WEATHER: warm humid cloudy weather | SOIL: deep black cotton vertisol | STAGE: square formation and flowering] ADVICE: Cloudy warm weather stimulates bollworm egg deposition on tender terminals. Erect pheromone traps at 12 traps/ha for monitoring. Spray chlorantraniliprole or novaluron at economic threshold of 1 egg or larva per plant.
[CROP: Cotton | CONTEXT: sucking pests aphids and thrips | WEATHER: dry hot weather, temperature 32-38C | SOIL: black soil | STAGE: vegetative 20-45 days] ADVICE: Thrips and aphids desiccate leaf margins during hot dry spells causing leaf cupping. Spray flonicamid 50 WG at 0.3 g/liter or diafenthiuron 50 WP. Conserve natural predators by intercropping with cowpea border rows.
[CROP: Cotton | CONTEXT: verticillium and fusarium wilt | WEATHER: cool wet soil followed by sudden hot sun | SOIL: poorly drained clay soil | STAGE: flowering to boll opening] ADVICE: Soilborne vascular wilts strike when waterlogged soils abruptly encounter high heat. Deep summer plow to solarize soil, rotate with sorghum, and drench affected patches with carbendazim combined with Trichoderma viride.
[CROP: Cotton | CONTEXT: square and boll shedding | WEATHER: cloudy overcast skies for several days | SOIL: all types | STAGE: peak flowering] ADVICE: Prolonged low solar radiation reduces photosynthetic carbohydrate supply, inducing natural boll drop. Spray 2% diammonium phosphate (DAP) combined with 1% potassium sulfate and 10 ppm planofix NAA to curb shedding.

[CROP: Soybean | CONTEXT: Asian soybean rust Phakopsora | WEATHER: continuous rains, cloudiness, temperature 20-25C | SOIL: loamy clay | STAGE: reproductive stages R1 to R3] ADVICE: Fungal spores require at least 6 hours of leaf wetness to penetrate. Apply strobilurin plus triazole premix fungicide (such as azoxystrobin + difenoconazole) at first flowering if rainfall persists.
[CROP: Soybean | CONTEXT: poor nodulation nitrogen failure | WEATHER: hot dry seedbed, temperature >35C | SOIL: sandy soil low in organic matter | STAGE: sowing] ADVICE: Bradyrhizobium nodulation bacteria die when exposed to hot dry soil and direct sunlight. Inoculate seeds under shade with fresh peat culture plus molybdenum, sow within 4 hours, and maintain seedbed moisture.
[CROP: Soybean | CONTEXT: pod borer and girdle beetle | WEATHER: humid warm overcast weather | SOIL: black soil | STAGE: pod filling] ADVICE: Girdle beetle females girdle stems during humid spells causing plant tops to wilt. Spray chlorantraniliprole 18.5 SC at 150 ml/ha or indoxacarb 14.5 SC at the onset of stem ring cuts.
[CROP: Soybean | CONTEXT: iron deficiency chlorosis IDC | WEATHER: cool wet spring | SOIL: calcareous high pH soil >7.5 with free calcium carbonate | STAGE: V2 to V4 vegetative] ADVICE: Cold wet soils exacerbate bicarbonate buildup preventing iron uptake, resulting in bright yellow leaves with green veins. Band Fe-EDDHA chelated iron in furrow at planting and avoid excessive early tillage.

[CROP: Chilli | CONTEXT: thrips and mite leaf curl | WEATHER: hot dry weather for thrips, warm humid for mites | SOIL: well-drained loamy soil | STAGE: flowering and fruiting] ADVICE: Upward leaf curling indicates thrips whereas downward curling signifies yellow mite damage. Spray spinosad 45 SC at 0.3 ml/liter for thrips or propargite 57 EC at 2 ml/liter for mites.
[CROP: Chilli | CONTEXT: anthracnose fruit rot dieback | WEATHER: warm rainy spells, temperature 28-30C, humidity >80% | SOIL: loam | STAGE: ripening fruit stage] ADVICE: Rain splashes fungal spores from infected twigs onto maturing fruits causing circular sunken lesions. Spray azoxystrobin 23 SC or copper oxychloride 50 WP and harvest ripe fruits frequently to curb inoculum buildup.
[CROP: Chilli | CONTEXT: flower drop under temperature extremes | WEATHER: night temperature >24C or day temperature >38C | SOIL: sandy loam | STAGE: flowering] ADVICE: Temperature spikes cause pollen desiccation and stigma drying. Mulch rows with silver-black reflective film to lower root zone temperature and spray 20 ppm naphthalene acetic acid at 10-day intervals.

[CROP: Onion | CONTEXT: purple blotch and Stemphylium | WEATHER: warm humid conditions, intermittent showers, temperature 25C | SOIL: sandy loam | STAGE: bulb development] ADVICE: Purple blotch destroys photosynthetic leaf area reducing bulb sizing. Spray tebuconazole 25.9 EC at 1 ml/liter or mancozeb at 2.5 g/liter mixed with a wetting agent (sticker) to ensure adhesion on waxy onion leaves.
[CROP: Onion | CONTEXT: onion thrips infestation | WEATHER: hot dry climate, rainfall deficiency | SOIL: rich well-drained loam | STAGE: vegetative to bulb initiation] ADVICE: Thrips rasp onion leaves creating silvery white patches. Apply spinetoram 11.7 SC or fipronil 5 SC. Ensure overhead sprinkler irrigation twice a week as water droplets physically wash thrips from leaf axils.
[CROP: Onion | CONTEXT: bulb splitting and double bulbs | WEATHER: temperature fluctuations, erratic watering | SOIL: heavy clay soil | STAGE: bulb enlargement] ADVICE: Fluctuations between drought and sudden heavy irrigation cause outer scales to split and produce unmarketable double bulbs. Maintain steady soil moisture through controlled drip intervals.

[CROP: Groundnut | CONTEXT: tikka leaf spot Cercospora | WEATHER: warm cloudy weather, temperature 25-28C, high humidity | SOIL: sandy loam | STAGE: pegging and pod formation] ADVICE: Tikka leaf spots cause rapid defoliation under warm cloudy conditions. Spray hexaconazole 5 SC at 2 ml/liter or chlorothalonil 75 WP at 2 g/liter at first sign of circular dark spots with yellow halos.
[CROP: Groundnut | CONTEXT: aflatoxin contamination Aspergillus | WEATHER: end of season drought, high soil temperature >30C | SOIL: light sandy soil | STAGE: pod maturity and harvest] ADVICE: Drought stress during the last 3 to 4 weeks of pod maturation cracks pod shells allowing Aspergillus flavus fungus to enter. Provide terminal irrigation if possible, dry harvested pods immediately to below 8% moisture, and never heap moist vines.
[CROP: Groundnut | CONTEXT: collar rot Sclerotium | WEATHER: hot humid weather after sowing, wet seedbed | SOIL: clayey soil with poor drainage | STAGE: seedling emergence] ADVICE: Seedling collar rot rots subterranean hypocotyls. Treat seeds with Trichoderma harzianum at 10 g/kg seed plus captan before sowing and avoid deep seed placement beyond 5 cm depth.

[CROP: Apple | CONTEXT: apple scab Venturia | WEATHER: prolonged spring rains, temperature 16-24C, leaves wet for >9 hours | SOIL: loam | STAGE: green tip to petal fall] ADVICE: Mills infection tables dictate that 9 hours of continuous leaf wetness at 18C triggers scab infection. Spray protective captan 50 WP or dodine before forecast rain events and follow with difenoconazole or kresoxim-methyl if rain exceeds 24 hours.
[CROP: Apple | CONTEXT: codling moth worm in fruit | WEATHER: warm twilight evenings, temperature >15C | SOIL: well-drained mountain soil | STAGE: fruit development] ADVICE: Female codling moths lay single eggs on developing fruitlets on calm warm evenings. Monitor pheromone trap catches to calculate degree-days and spray chlorantraniliprole or apply codling moth granulovirus (CpGV) at egg hatch.
[CROP: Apple | CONTEXT: spring frost blossom kill | WEATHER: clear sky radiation frost, temperature <-2C | SOIL: gravelly loam | STAGE: tight cluster to full bloom] ADVICE: Open apple blossoms die when temperatures drop below minus 2.2 degrees Celsius. Operate overhead frost protection sprinklers continuously throughout the freeze event until ice begins to melt naturally.
[CROP: Apple | CONTEXT: bitter pit calcium deficiency | WEATHER: hot dry summers, excessive vegetative growth | SOIL: acidic gravelly soil | STAGE: 4 to 8 weeks after petal fall] ADVICE: High tree vigor under hot dry weather channels calcium to leaves instead of fruits causing corky bitter pit spots. Apply 4 to 6 foliar sprays of calcium chloride at 0.5% at fortnightly intervals and avoid heavy nitrogen fertilization.

[CROP: Coffee | CONTEXT: coffee leaf rust Hemileia | WEATHER: monsoon rains, warm humid weather, temperature 22-26C | SOIL: fertile volcanic red soil | STAGE: post-blossom to berry development] ADVICE: Rust urediniospores disperse through rain splashes and require 24 to 48 hours of humidity to infect lower leaf stomata. Spray preventive 0.5% Bordeaux mixture before monsoon arrival followed by epoxiconazole or copper oxychloride at first symptoms.
[CROP: Coffee | CONTEXT: coffee berry borer Hypothenemus | WEATHER: intermittent light showers during berry ripening | SOIL: organic rich forest soil | STAGE: berry maturation] ADVICE: Berry borers bore into the navel of green and ripening coffee cherries. Install red alcohol-baited funnel traps at 20 traps per hectare and implement clean strip harvesting leaving no fallen or dried berries on bushes or ground.
[CROP: Coffee | CONTEXT: shade regulation microclimate control | WEATHER: excessive solar radiation, high temperature >30C | SOIL: mountain slope loam | STAGE: all seasons] ADVICE: Regulate two-tier shade tree canopy to maintain 35 to 45% filtered sunlight. Balanced shade lowers canopy temperature by up to 4 degrees, buffers humidity drops, and suppresses leaf rust epidemics.

[CROP: Sugarcane | CONTEXT: red rot Colletotrichum | WEATHER: waterlogging, high humidity >85%, temperature 28-32C | SOIL: poorly drained alluvial clay | STAGE: grand growth phase] ADVICE: Red rot causes internal stalk reddening with white cross-patches and sudden crown wilting. Rogue out diseased clumps completely with roots, burn them outside the field, ensure quick drainage of standing rainwater, and never cultivate ratoon crops in infected plots.
[CROP: Sugarcane | CONTEXT: early shoot borer Chilo | WEATHER: hot dry weather with high temperature >38C, low humidity | SOIL: sandy loam | STAGE: germination to 90 days] ADVICE: Early shoot borer kills central growing shoots creating foul-smelling dead hearts during hot dry months. Apply chlorpyrifos 20 EC or fipronil 0.3 G granules in the furrows, cover with soil, and conduct light earthing-up at 45 days after planting.
[CROP: Sugarcane | CONTEXT: moisture conservation under drought | WEATHER: severe summer drought, scarce irrigation | SOIL: light loam | STAGE: formative phase] ADVICE: Spread dry sugarcane trash mulch at 4 to 5 tons per hectare between cane rows to cut evaporation losses by 40%, lower soil temperature, and prevent weed germination during dry spells.

[CROP: Banana | CONTEXT: sigatoka leaf spot | WEATHER: heavy rains, high humidity >90%, temperature 25-28C | SOIL: deep fertile alluvial soil | STAGE: vegetative to shooting] ADVICE: Black sigatoka destroys functional leaves severely reducing bunch weight. De-leaf severely spotted leaves promptly to reduce airborne ascospores and spray propiconazole 25 EC or mineral oil emulsion at 10-day intervals during the rainy season.
[CROP: Banana | CONTEXT: wind damage and pseudo-stem snapping | WEATHER: gusty winds, squalls, pre-monsoon storm alerts | SOIL: clay loam | STAGE: bunch development] ADVICE: Heavy banana bunches make tall plants vulnerable to wind snapping. Provide sturdy bamboo or eucalyptus props in an inverted V-shape to each fruiting plant and plant windbreak casuarina trees on field borders.
